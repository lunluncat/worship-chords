    Guitar chord lesson

    How to read a chord diagram

    • The vertical lines are the six strings, from the thickest (low E) on the left to the thinnest (high E) on the right.
    • The horizontal lines are frets. A filled dot means press that string down at that fret.
    • A × above a string means do not play it. A small circle means play it open, with no finger.
    • A thick bar across several strings is a barre: lay one finger flat across all of them.
    • A thick line at the top is the nut, so the diagram starts at the first fret. A label such as 5fr means the diagram starts at the 5th fret.
    • The numbers under each chord give the fret for each string, low E to high E. An x means muted, so x32010 is the C chord.

    Practice tips

    • Press with your fingertips, close behind the fret wire, so every string rings clearly.
    • Pick each string one at a time and fix any buzzing before you speed up.
    • Practise changing between two chords, like G and C, for a minute at a time.
    • For barre chords, roll your index finger slightly onto its bony side and squeeze with the thumb behind the neck.
    • Barre shapes slide up and down the neck. The same shape one fret higher is the chord one semitone higher.
